-   **Stop Signs**: In the context of reading the Quran, stop signs are known as **Waqf** symbols (*Al-Waqf* means "the stop"). Because classical Arabic lacks modern punctuation like periods or commas, these small letters placed above the words serve as a guide. They tell you where you *must* stop, where you *can* stop, and where you *should absolutely not* stop to avoid altering the sacred meaning of the text.

    1. **The Compulsory Stop**: **مـ** (*Waqf Lazim*)

        * **What it means:** **Must stop.**
        * **Why:** If you continue reading past this sign without pausing, it will drastically change or distort the intended meaning of the sentence.
        * **Action:** Take a full stop and a breath before continuing.

    2. **The Prohibited Stop**: **لا** (*Waqf Mamnoo’*)

        * **What it means:** **Do not stop.**
        * **Why:** Stopping here breaks the sentence structure midway, which would result in an incomplete or incorrect meaning.
        * **Action:** Keep reading. *Note: If you accidentally run out of breath here, you should backtrack a word or two and re-read the passage to ensure continuity.*

    3. **The Better-to-Stop Sign**: **قلي** (*Waqf Al-Awla*)

        * **What it means:** **Stopping is preferred.**
        * **Action:** While it is perfectly permissible to connect the text and keep reading, pausing here is highly recommended and usually yields a cleaner recitation and clearer understanding of the verse.

    4. **The Better-to-Continue Sign**: **صلي** (*Al-Wasl Al-Awla*)

        * **What it means:** **Continuing is preferred.**
        * **Action:** This is the opposite of *qali*. While you are legally allowed to stop and take a breath if you need to, it is much better to link this phrase to the next word without pausing.

    5. **The Optional Stop**: **ج** (*Waqf Ja’iz*)

        * **What it means:** **Equal choice.**
        * **Action:** This represents a neutral pause. The meaning remains perfectly intact whether you choose to stop and take a breath or continue reading smoothly through it.

    6. **The Linked/Embracing Stop**: **؞   ؞** (*Waqf Ta’anuq*)

        * **What it means:** **Stop at one, but not both.**
        * **Action:** These look like two triplets of dots appearing close to each other in a verse. You have a choice: you can stop at the first set of dots *or* you can stop at the second set of dots. However, you cannot stop at both, and you should not skip both.

    7. **Other Common Marginal Symbols**

        * **The End of a Verse (۝):** This stylized circle contains the verse number. It is an Sunnah (prophetic tradition) and universally accepted practice to stop at the end of any verse, resetting your breath for the next one.
        * **س (Saktah):** This indicates a **breathless pause**. You must stop your voice for a brief moment (about one second), but you *do not* take a new breath before continuing.
